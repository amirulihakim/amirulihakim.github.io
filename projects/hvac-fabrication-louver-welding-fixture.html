HVAC Fabrication & Louver Welding Fixture Design

Manufacturing engineering internship focused on HVAC sheet-metal fabrication and louver production, followed by the development of a proposed dedicated welding fixture for more repeatable frame and blade assembly.

Manufacturing Engineering Intern · Aug 2025 – Oct 2025

Engineering Summary

From fabrication observation to a dedicated fixture proposal.

Background
During my internship, I observed HVAC louver production from sheet-metal marking and shearing through bending, MIG assembly, finishing, and coating.
Problem
Frame positioning and blade alignment relied on repeated manual measurement and setup during assembly.
What I Built
I documented the production workflow and developed a CAD fixture concept with mechanical supports, locating features, and distributed manual clamps.
Result
The work produced process documentation and a dedicated welding-fixture proposal intended to support repeatable assembly. The fixture was not fabricated or production-tested.

Proposed Fixture

Proposed Louver Frame Welding Fixture

The fixture was developed as a dedicated assembly aid for the louver frame. The concept supports the long rectangular workpiece, provides repeatable positioning points along the assembly, and uses multiple clamping locations to hold the sheet-metal components during tack welding and assembly.

Design StatusProposed concept — not implemented or production-tested

Manufacturing Context

Large fabricated louver assembly.

The product is a fabricated HVAC louver constructed from 1.2 mm HRC sheet steel. Its long, relatively thin assembly geometry makes consistent positioning important during frame and blade assembly.

  • 3580 mm overall length
  • 550 mm main louver height
  • 1.2 mm HRC / black steel sheet
  • 15 louver blades

Fixture Design Objective

Control positioning before welding.

Frame Support

Support the long louver assembly along its span instead of relying on temporary workshop positioning.

Repeatable Location

Provide consistent mechanical reference points for frame, divider, and blade positioning.

Quick Clamping

Use distributed manually operated clamps to restrain components during assembly and tack welding.

Weld Access

Keep the fixture sufficiently open so the operator can reach required weld locations.

Adjustability / Setup

Provide manually operated clamping and positioning features for workpiece setup and removal; this is a mechanical fixture concept, not an automated machine.

Mechanical Design

Fixture layout and workholding arrangement.

Workpiece Envelope
Fixture proportions follow the long louver-frame geometry, with distributed support along the assembly.
Clamping
Multiple manual clamps are positioned along the frame to restrain components during assembly.
Positioning
Mechanical locating and support features provide references beyond tape-measure positioning.
Accessibility
The open-frame construction retains access to the assembly from multiple sides.
Mobile Structure
The CAD concept uses a supported frame with wheeled bases for workshop mobility.
Support / Positioning Mechanism
The side view shows a central rotary support arrangement. Rotation range, load capacity, and locking performance remain unverified.

Design Status

A proposed fixture, not a validated production machine.

The fixture was developed as an engineering proposal following observation of the manufacturing process. The CAD model demonstrates the intended workholding and positioning concept, but the design was not fabricated or production-tested during the internship.

Further development would require verification of:

  • Structural stiffness
  • Clamp accessibility
  • Weld clearance
  • Fixture distortion under welding heat
  • Safe rotation and locking if the rotary concept is retained
  • Operator ergonomics
  • Manufacturability
  • Fabrication cost
  • Actual cycle-time impact
